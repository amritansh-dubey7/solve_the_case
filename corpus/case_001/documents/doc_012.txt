WITNESS STATEMENT — DR. WILLOUGHBY ANSTRUTHER, physician to Mr. John Turner
Taken by Constable Figgins at his surgery in Ross

Mr. Turner has been my patient for upward of two years for a progressive kidney complaint (Bright's disease). His condition has worsened markedly in the last six months. He suffers considerable pain, general weakness, and has developed a pronounced stiffness and swelling in his right leg and hip that has left him walking with a heavy limp, requiring a stout stick to get about at all, even indoors. He has, by my estimate, a matter of months to live, not years.

Despite his illness he retains a tall, powerful frame from his younger days and can, with effort and his stick, cover moderate distances on foot, though it costs him greatly and leaves him exhausted and in pain afterward. He is a heavy smoker of strong Trichinopoly cigars against my repeated advice, a habit I have been unable to persuade him to give up given his condition. He generally wears a heavy grey travelling-cloak out of doors even in mild weather, as his circulation is poor and he feels the cold badly.

I attended him on the afternoon of Mr. McCarthy's death and found him in considerable pain and unusually agitated and exhausted, more so than his illness alone would explain, though he offered no explanation and I did not press him, believing it not my place.
