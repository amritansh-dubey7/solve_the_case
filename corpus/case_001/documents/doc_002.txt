WITNESS STATEMENT
Witness: Patience Moran, age 14, daughter of the lodge-keeper of the Boscombe Valley estate
Taken by: Constable H. Figgins
Date: 3 June (afternoon of the death)

I was picking flowers in Boscombe Wood, near the pool, a little before four o'clock. I saw Mr. Charles McCarthy and young Mr. James McCarthy walking together toward the pool. They seemed to be quarrelling — I heard Mr. James McCarthy using very bad language to his father, and I saw him raise his hand as if he would strike him. I was frightened by the violence of it and ran home to tell my mother.

While I was telling her, I heard young Mr. McCarthy come running to the lodge, without a hat, and very disturbed, saying something dreadful had happened and asking for help. We — my mother, Mr. Crowder, and I — went back with him to the pool, and that is where we found old Mr. McCarthy, dying or already dead, on the ground near the water.

I could not hear what the two men were quarrelling about. I was too far to make out most of the words. I only heard young Mr. McCarthy say something that sounded like he was calling his father a name, and I am fairly sure I heard the word "Alice" in it, though I could not swear to the rest of what was said.
