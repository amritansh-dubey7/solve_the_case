WITNESS STATEMENT
Witness: William Crowder, gamekeeper, Boscombe Valley estate (employed by Mr. John Turner)
Taken by: Constable H. Figgins
Date: 3 June (afternoon of the death)

I was in the wood near Boscombe Pool that afternoon, about a hundred and fifty yards off, doing my rounds. Shortly before four o'clock I heard a man's voice cry out "Cooee!" — that is a call the old man used with his son, I'm told it's an Australian habit of theirs, as they both lived out there some years. I believe it was Mr. Charles McCarthy's voice, calling for his son to come to him at the pool.

A minute or two after, I heard raised voices from the direction of the pool, though I could not make out words at that distance. I did not think much of it at the time, as the two of them were known to quarrel.

Some minutes later, young Mr. James McCarthy came running toward me and the lodge, hatless and in great distress, saying his father was dead or dying at the pool. I went back with him, his mother — I mean Mrs. Moran and her daughter — and we found old Mr. McCarthy on the ground, his head badly injured, not far from the water's edge. He was still breathing but could not speak clearly. Young Mr. McCarthy was kneeling by him.

I saw no other person near the pool that afternoon, before or after, though the wood is thick with undergrowth and reeds along the bank, and a person could pass along the far side without my seeing them from where I stood.
