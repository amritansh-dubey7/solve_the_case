CONFIDENTIAL STATEMENT OF JOHN TURNER — TAKEN PRIVATELY BY MR. SHERLOCK HOLMES
Given at the Turner estate house, witnessed by Dr. Watson, not disclosed to the constabulary at the time of taking; retained by Holmes pending the outcome of the inquest.

I will tell you the truth, Mr. Holmes, since you have plainly worked it all out already and there is no profit in denying it to a dying man.

More than twenty years ago, in the colony of Victoria, Australia, I was a very different man than I am now — I rode with a gang of bushrangers, and was known there as Black Jack of Ballarat. We held up a gold convoy. In the course of it a man was killed by another of our party, though I did not strike the blow myself, I will not pretend my hands were clean of the whole affair. Charles McCarthy was one of the convoy's guards, and he alone among them recognized me clearly enough, years later, to know me again when we both, by the most bitter chance, came to settle as neighbours here in Herefordshire, he as my tenant.

From the day he knew me he held that knowledge over my head. I gave him land on easy terms, money whenever he asked it, and asked nothing but silence in return, and for years that was enough for him. But these last months he grew bolder and crueler in his demands, and at last he told me plainly that nothing would satisfy him now but my Alice's marriage to his son James, so that through her, and through my land and fortune, his blood would rise in the world at my expense — regardless of what she or James wished, regardless of their own wishes in the matter, which is a cruelty I could not permit even to save myself.

I met him at the pool as he asked. I begged him one last time to release me and Alice from this. He refused, and mocked me, and turned to walk away as though the matter were settled and beneath further discussion. Something in me broke at that. I struck him with a heavy stone from the bank — more than once, I am ashamed to say, though I could not tell you how many times in that moment. I dropped the stone in the reeds and left him by the pool, thinking, God forgive me, that I had done what was needed.

I am dying, Mr. Holmes, of my own body's failing, whatever the law might otherwise have done to me. I ask only that James McCarthy, who is innocent of this and has suffered enough for the sins of his father and of mine, be spared.
