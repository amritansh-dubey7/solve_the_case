INQUEST TESTIMONY — JAMES McCARTHY
Given before the coroner, Ross-on-Wye
Transcribed by court clerk

I had been away at Bristol for three days and returned that morning without telling my father the exact time, as I did not know it myself until the night before. I took my gun and went out toward the rabbit warren near Boscombe Pool in the afternoon.

As I came through the wood I heard my father's call — "Cooee!" — which was a signal between us, a habit from our years in Victoria, Australia. I went to him at the pool. He seemed surprised and, I think, displeased to see me there, and asked rather sharply what I was doing. We fell into a quarrel. I will not pretend it was not bitter. He wished me to do something I could not honourably do, concerning my attachment to Miss Alice Turner, and pressed me in a manner I found intolerable. I raised my hand — I own that much, to my shame — but I did not strike him, and I turned and left him alive and standing by the pool.

I had gone perhaps a hundred and fifty yards when I heard a cry behind me — not the "Cooee" call, but a cry of distress, inarticulate. I ran back and found my father on the ground, grievously wounded about the head, and I could not say by what. I had left my gun leaning on a tree some yards off when I first went to him, as was my habit, so as not to hold it while talking with my father. I knelt by him. He tried to speak but I could make out only a broken word or two before he died — it sounded like "a rat," repeated, though I could make nothing sensible of it, and one further sound I took for a name but could not place.

I did not see any other person near the pool, before or after, though I confess I was not looking — my attention was entirely on my father, both during our quarrel and after I found him dying.

I told Mr. Crowder at first that I had not seen my father that afternoon. That was a lie, and I regret it, but it was told from shame at the quarrel and its subject, not from guilt of the deed.
