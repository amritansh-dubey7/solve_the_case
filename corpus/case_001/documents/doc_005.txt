SURGEON'S REPORT ON THE BODY OF CHARLES McCARTHY
Prepared by the district medical examiner for the coroner's inquest

Cause of death: severe blunt-force trauma to the posterior and lateral skull, consistent with repeated blows from a heavy object. The wounds are not consistent with a firearm, a knife, or a thin implement such as a walking-stick alone; the injuries suggest a blow or blows delivered with considerable force from a heavy, somewhat rounded object.

The deceased was found partially in the shallow water at the pool's edge. Time of death is estimated between 3:30 and 4:15 p.m. on the day in question, consistent with witness accounts.

No defensive wounds were noted on the deceased's hands or forearms, suggesting the fatal blow(s) may have been struck without significant struggle, or from a position or angle the deceased did not anticipate.

The deceased was able to make some sound after the injury, per witness accounts, consistent with survival for a brief period — likely no more than one to two minutes — before death, sufficient time to have attempted speech, though any words would likely have been indistinct given the nature of the injury.
